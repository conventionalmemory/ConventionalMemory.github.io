Retro PC starter kit: the gear worth buying

You found a beige box at a garage sale. Congratulations, you now own a project. Here is the short list of gear that is worth the money, and the stuff that is not.

Connie: “Rule one of vintage computers: it will not work the first time. Rule two: that is the fun part.”

How we pick. A product makes the list only when reviewers and long-time collectors keep recommending it, it does one job well and it will still work in five years. No filler, no mystery brands, and no paid placements. Not every item has been through our own bench, so we go by reputation. Prices change, so check the listing.

Storage that outlasts the original

Old hard drives die. Floppies rot. These picks let a 30-year-old machine keep going without stress.

CompactFlash card and IDE adapter

Hard drive replacement

A CompactFlash card in a 40 pin IDE adapter acts as a hard disk on most 486 and Pentium machines. Keep the card small (a few gigabytes is plenty) because old BIOSes get confused by big drives.

Watch out: Adapters are all alike, so pick one with good recent reviews. Use an industrial-grade card from Delkin or Transcend, not a mystery card.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

Gotek floppy emulator with FlashFloppy firmware

Floppy drive that reads a USB stick

A Gotek swaps a flaky floppy drive for a USB stick full of disk images. The free, open-source FlashFloppy firmware is the community standard, so plan to flash it if yours ships with something else.

Watch out: Some units ship with odd firmware. Check the listing, then flash FlashFloppy. For a premium option, look at the HxC Floppy Emulator.

Greaseweazle

Floppy disk imager

An open hardware board that reads and writes real floppies on a modern PC, down to the magnetic flux, so you can rescue and keep your old disks before they go.

Watch out: Skip the cheap USB floppy readers for this job. Many old formats are beyond them.

Power and reading

Two small things that save big headaches.

A good surge protector

APC or Tripp Lite

A 30-year-old machine with an aging power supply should not be plugged straight into the wall. Pick an APC or Tripp Lite protector with a switch, so you can turn it all off at once.

Check Amazon

Reference book

The long-running reference for how PCs fit together, from the 8088 onward. Older editions cover the machines you actually own.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

Skip these

  • Bags of mixed capacitors from no-name sellers. Buy a known brand, in the exact value your board needs.
  • Cheap USB floppy readers for rescuing old disks. Use a Greaseweazle, or a Gotek for just playing disks.
  • Machines sold without a photo of the motherboard battery area. That is where the nasty surprises live.
  • Anything with a thousand five-star reviews and no brand. Retro gear is a small market, so the good stuff has a name behind it.
