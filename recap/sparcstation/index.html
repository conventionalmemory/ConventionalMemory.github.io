Which board do I have?

Boards changed during production. These notes come from the linked sources, and many are only partly documented, so check the board in front of you.

  • SPARCstation IPC and IPX supply (Medium confidence). How to tell: The IPC and IPX share the same power supply. Capacitors: Replacements fitted in an IPX: 470uF 25V, 3300uF 10V, 47uF 50V (some originals were 25V), 1uF 50V, 220uF 16V, 100uF 25V, 1000uF 25V. Capacitor list differs from other revisions: not known. Source
  • SPARCstation 20 supply FDK PEX668-31 (Medium confidence). How to tell: FDK part number as given on the forum. Capacitors: Output side caps C32, C35, C27, C29 fail first; the poster advises replacing all aluminum electrolytics with low impedance parts. Capacitor list differs from other revisions: not known. Source

Tips, tales and maybes

Found in write-ups and forum threads. These are other people's reports, not tested by us. Check part references and values against your own board.

  • Tip: IPX PSU caps and the NVRAM battery (Medium confidence). A DesignSpark restoration of an IPX replaced the failed PSU electrolytics (a mix from 1uF 50V up to 3300uF 10V) and then dealt with the battery-backed NVRAM, which holds the MAC address and hostid. The author kept the historical MAC from system logs to reprogram the ID. Source
  • Watch out: SPARCstation 20 supply: C32, C35, C27, C29 (Medium confidence). A post on the FDK PEX668-31 supply names output filter caps C32, C35, C27 and C29 as venting with age, warns that the input caps can store a lethal charge, and says to use matched low-impedance parts. It also advises recording the EADDR and serial before the NVRAM battery dies. Source
  • Story: IPC: soft click, green LED flash, nothing (Medium confidence). A Sun IPC thread describes a supply that gives a soft click and a quick green LED flash, usually from the three tallest 3300uF caps leaking. The recommended parts were 105C low-ESR types such as Panasonic FR, with a thorough alcohol clean afterwards. Source

Before you start

  • Boards changed during production. Check your board number against the guides before you order parts.
  • A mains power supply can hold a dangerous charge. If you are not trained to work on one, have a professional do it.
