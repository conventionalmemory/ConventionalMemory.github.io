Which board do I have?

Boards changed during production. These notes come from the linked sources, and many are only partly documented, so check the board in front of you.

  • ThinkPad 700C (Low confidence). How to tell: Labelled 700C; the series also includes the mono 700, 720 and 710T. Capacitors: On one 700C the only electrolytics on the main system were three on the DC/DC board; a leaking one was a surface mount 47uF. Capacitor list differs from other revisions: not known. Where to start

Clues from write-ups and forum threads, not a diagnosis. Low confidence means one thread or one repair; high means several write-ups agree.

  • DC/DC board (Low confidence). One write-up notes just three electrolytics on the DC/DC board, including a leaking 47 uF SMD cap. Source

Tips, tales and maybes

Found in write-ups and forum threads. These are other people's reports, not tested by us. Check part references and values against your own board.

  • Tip: Where the 700 series caps are (Medium confidence). A thread on 700, 700C, 720 and 720C machines lists leaking electrolytics on three boards: the display inverter (3 caps), the contrast/VCOM board (5 caps, often unmarked) and the main DC/DC board (3 caps). Members used 22uF 35V, 47uF 25V and 100uF 10V. A screaming noise from the base is a reported symptom. Source
  • Watch out: Hinge mounts crack in storage (Low confidence). The same thread says the brittle plastic hinge mounts fail when the lid stays attached to the base in storage, and suggests separating them. The 700 series also uses proprietary ESDI drives, so a drive error may not be a capacitor issue. Source
  • Story: 750c backlight and LCD died, leaking SMD caps (Low confidence). A 750c owner found leaking SMD caps on two power boards feeding the CCFL, and the LCD later died too. A reply said failed backlights on 750s are usually leaking caps on the LCD and inverter boards and a recap probably fixes them. Source
  • Tip: 701c memory errors: try the CMOS battery first (Medium confidence). A thread on error codes 201, 161 and 163 on a 701c says nearly all such memory errors were a weak CMOS battery. The author measured a depleted cell causing error 201 and a reversed cell giving beeps, so replace the coin cell before blaming RAM or caps. Source
