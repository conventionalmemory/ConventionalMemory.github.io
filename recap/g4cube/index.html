Which board do I have?

Boards changed during production. These notes come from the linked sources, and many are only partly documented, so check the board in front of you.

  • G4 Cube power brick (Low confidence). How to tell: Marked designators C1, C2, C6, C15 and C33. Capacitors: Five electrolytics; C15 is most likely to fail and C6 second. The thread's value readings look like measured, not nominal, so read the originals off the board. Capacitor list differs from other revisions: not known. Source

Where to start

Clues from write-ups and forum threads, not a diagnosis. Low confidence means one thread or one repair; high means several write-ups agree.

  • Power brick caps (Medium confidence). One thread measured the original values and lists replacements for C1, C2, C6, C15 and C33, with disassembly steps. Source

Tips, tales and maybes

Found in write-ups and forum threads. These are other people's reports, not tested by us. Check part references and values against your own board.

  • Tip: Which caps fail first on the brick (Medium confidence). A restorer's thread on the Cube brick says C15 and C6 are the most failure-prone and deserve robust replacements, and that replacing all the electrolytics is better than one. Measure the output (about 28V no load) after soldering the shield plates and before closing the cage. Source
  • Watch out: Opening the brick breaks things (Medium confidence). Posters report the plastic end cap tabs break even after drilling the holes, metal cage tabs may need cutters, and the silicone holding the plates takes effort to separate without tearing solder points. One cap's negative lead must keep its stock length because it solders to both the board and copper plates. Source

Before you start

  • Boards changed during production. Check your board number against the guides before you order parts.
  • A mains power supply can hold a dangerous charge. If you are not trained to work on one, have a professional do it.
