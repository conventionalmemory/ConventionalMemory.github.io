Which board do I have?

Boards changed during production. These notes come from the linked sources, and many are only partly documented, so check the board in front of you.

  • Astec supply (Model B) (Low confidence). How to tell: Maker name shown on the supply. Capacitors: A UK kit lists C2 100nF X2 x2, C1 10nF X2 x2 and C9 220uF 25V 105C for all supplies it covers. Capacitor list differs from other revisions: no. Source
  • BSR supply (Model B) (Low confidence). How to tell: Maker name shown on the supply. Capacitors: Same single kit listed. Capacitor list differs from other revisions: no. Source
  • Hitron supply (Master) (Low confidence). How to tell: Maker name shown on the supply. Capacitors: Same single kit listed. Capacitor list differs from other revisions: no. Source

Where to start

Clues from write-ups and forum threads, not a diagnosis. Low confidence means one thread or one repair; high means several write-ups agree.

  • X2 line-filter caps (High confidence). Several sources point at the two PSU X2 caps (100 nF and 10 nF) as the ones to replace. Source
  • Starter electrolytic (High confidence). Kit and how-to pages name the 220 uF 25V electrolytic C9 as the other power supply part to replace. Source

Tips, tales and maybes

Found in write-ups and forum threads. These are other people's reports, not tested by us. Check part references and values against your own board.

  • Watch out: Warning signs of the X2 caps (Medium confidence). A kit seller lists an acrid smell, hairline cracks or split resin on the X2 caps as signs of imminent failure, and says the caps are only noise suppression, so the machine runs without them. It cites a 100nF and a 10nF part. Source
  • Story: Cracked Rifa caps on arrival (Low confidence). One restoration found both Rifa suppression caps cracked and the small one slightly bulging, and the author says near-40-year-old ones rarely last long once powered. The caps were replaced before the first power-up. Source
  • Tip: Three classic BBC supply faults (Medium confidence). A BBC repair page lists a dry startup cap C9 (220uF 25V 105C, strained sound at switch-on and 5V below 1V), a failed chopper transistor Q2 (BU208A on the B, 2SC2928 on the Master), and smoking X2 caps C2 (100nF) and C1 (10nF), 250VAC, 15mm pitch. It warns the voltages inside are potentially lethal. Source
  • Tip: Smell tells you which cap (Low confidence). A forum post on a smoking BBC says nasty smoke means the Rifa, while a merely unpleasant smell means a dried-out electrolytic. The owner fixed it with local capacitors and was pointed to a kit seller. Source

Before you start

  • Boards changed during production. Check your board number against the guides before you order parts.
  • A mains power supply can hold a dangerous charge. If you are not trained to work on one, have a professional do it.
