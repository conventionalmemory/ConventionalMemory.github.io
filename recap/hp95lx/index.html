Recap guide

Recap the HP 95LX, 100LX and 200LX

These are mechanical-first machines: broken case latches, cracked hinges (especially on the 200LX), screen columns dropping out and dirty keyboard contacts are the common faults. The one capacitor story is a 95LX with a false low-battery reading caused by a single 0.1uF tantalum.

Tips, tales and maybes

Found in write-ups and forum threads. These are other people's reports, not tested by us. Check part references and values against your own board.

  • Tip: 95LX false low battery (Medium confidence). A 95LX that reports low battery with fresh cells and an empty meter was traced to a 0.1uF tantalum just above the blue resistor pack, right of the lower RAM chip. A 0.1uF ceramic chip part replaced it, with a warning that this swap is only right for that one capacitor. Source
  • Tip: Latch and hinge failures (Medium confidence). The plastic latch springs break easily, and 200LX hinges crack from repeated opening. The repair page lists glue, reinforcement or easing the hinge spring, and says never to fully strip the right-hand hinge because it is almost impossible to rebuild. Source
  • Tip: Missing pixels or columns (Medium confidence). Missing screen pixels or columns often come from loose IC solder joints on the screen board or poor ribbon and cable contacts to the motherboard. Missing rows point at the ribbon cable. Source
  • Tip: Dead keys (Medium confidence). Keys that do not respond (menu, shift, zero, dot were named) may need their pressure contacts tightened, and the graphite keyboard foil contacts can be cleaned gently with alcohol. Source
  • Watch out: Battery polarity (Low confidence). A 95LX repair note cautions that even one battery inserted backwards can destroy the motherboard, so check polarity every time. Source
  • Story: Empty battery meter on a 95LX (Low confidence). A forum owner whose 95LX battery meter always read empty was pointed to the capacitor fix article and needed a finer soldering tip to do it. Another member offered to send parts. Source
  • Story: Most units still work (Low confidence). A museum catalogue entry for the 100LX says over 95 percent of the units its owners see are fully working and still very reliable. Source

Before you start

  • Boards changed during production. Check your board number against the guides before you order parts.
  • A mains power supply can hold a dangerous charge. If you are not trained to work on one, have a professional do it.
