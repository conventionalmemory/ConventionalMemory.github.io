Which board do I have?

Boards changed during production. These notes come from the linked sources, and many are only partly documented, so check the board in front of you.

  • A300 and A400 series (Medium confidence). How to tell: Early models with off board AA battery cells. Capacitors: Tantalum 12V decoupling cap C37, 100uF 16V, fails short; fit 100uF 25V. Capacitor list differs from other revisions: yes. Source
  • A400/1 series (Medium confidence). How to tell: Off board AA battery cells. Capacitors: Same fault cap is C53 on the board, 100uF 25V. Capacitor list differs from other revisions: yes. Source
  • A540, A3000, A5000, A3010, A3020, A4000 (Medium confidence). How to tell: Soldered rechargeable battery. Capacitors: Battery leak is the main problem; the Buyer's Guide says caps are rarely an issue except the A4, and some A5000 had caps fitted in reverse. Capacitor list differs from other revisions: not known. Source

Where to start

Clues from write-ups and forum threads, not a diagnosis. Low confidence means one thread or one repair; high means several write-ups agree.

  • 12V tantalum and RIFAs (Medium confidence). One common-faults page names a failing 12V tantalum decoupling cap (C37 or C53, by model) and early power supply RIFA X2 caps. Source
  • Caps rarely the problem (Low confidence). A buyer's guide says caps are rarely a problem apart from RIFAs, and that battery leaks are common. Source

Tips, tales and maybes

Found in write-ups and forum threads. These are other people's reports, not tested by us. Check part references and values against your own board.

  • Tip: Dead-looking A300/A400 from one decoupling cap (Medium confidence). A seller's fault guide says a faulty decoupling cap can make an A300/A400 look lifeless (C37, 100uF 16V on the A300/A400 series; C53, 100uF 25V on the A400/1). Their test is to remove the suspect cap and power up, and they suggest fitting a 100uF 25V part on all affected models. Source
  • Watch out: Battery leak outranks capacitors on later machines (Medium confidence). An A3000 restoration was driven by a leaking NiCd battery that damaged traces, vias and sockets, with symptoms like no keyboard present and a CMOS RAM error. The author still replaced the radial electrolytics (6x 220uF/16V, 2x 100uF/25V, 4x 47uF/25V, 8x 10uF/16V, 1x 4.7uF/63V) but says the list can differ between board revisions. Source
  • Tip: Do not power up a machine with battery damage (Low confidence). A repairer advises not applying power to a suspected battery-damaged Archimedes until it has been repaired, to avoid frying something that would have survived. The same A3000 had C15 rattling loose and needed top-side soldering. Source
  • Watch out: Assume battery damage; odd cap faults (Medium confidence). ArcWiki's buyer's guide says any battery not already removed will have leaked, usually destroying the CMOS RAM chip, except on 300-series, 440 and 400/1 machines with off-board cells. It adds that A4s need a recap, some A5000s had electrolytics fitted backwards, a RiscPC VCO cap can cause wobbly video, and early supplies with RIFA X2 caps should be replaced. Source
  • Story: A5000 caps about half bad (Medium confidence). An A5000 restoration ESR-tested its caps and found about half bad, some open circuit, with leakage in the audio area and broken traces under the pads. A new CMOS chip also cured other odd behaviour. Source

Before you start

  • Boards changed during production. Check your board number against the guides before you order parts.
  • A mains power supply can hold a dangerous charge. If you are not trained to work on one, have a professional do it.
