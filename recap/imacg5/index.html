Which board do I have?

Boards changed during production. These notes come from the linked sources, and many are only partly documented, so check the board in front of you.

  • iMac G5 Rev A (Medium confidence). How to tell: First revision. Capacitors: Bulging caps on the logic board causing shutdowns. Capacitor list differs from other revisions: not known. Source
  • iMac G5 Rev B (EMC 2056) (Medium confidence). How to tell: EMC number on the serial label under the stand; 20 inch Rev B is 2056. Capacitors: Logic board and or power supply failures; Apple ran a repair extension from Nov 2006. Capacitor list differs from other revisions: not known. Source
  • iMac G5 Rev C (iSight) (Low confidence). How to tell: Redesigned case with heat parts at the top. Capacitors: The threads do not report a cap problem for Rev C. Capacitor list differs from other revisions: not known. Source

Where to start

Clues from write-ups and forum threads, not a diagnosis. Low confidence means one thread or one repair; high means several write-ups agree.

  • Bulging caps (Low confidence). One iFixit story confirms the failure pattern on the power supply and logic board, but the machine was fixed by swapping the PSU, not by recapping. Source

Tips, tales and maybes

Found in write-ups and forum threads. These are other people's reports, not tested by us. Check part references and values against your own board.

  • Tip: Fuzzy boot screen: check 24 caps, then the GPU (Medium confidence). An iFixit answer for an A1058 with a distorted screen that clears after boot says to remove the bottom frame and inspect the 24 capacitors for bulging or corrosion, and to replace all the bad ones. It also offers a GPU solder joint reflow as the alternative cause. Source
  • Tip: Flat tops only and swap the PSU to test (Medium confidence). A thread says healthy cap tops should be perfectly flat and even a slight dome means a bad one. The owner's machine cutting out mid-boot was fixed by replacing the power supply, and the advice was to test with a known-good supply because both parts can fail independently. Source
  • Watch out: Good-looking caps prove nothing on a G5 (Low confidence). A MacRumors reply said nice-looking capacitors are no proof they have not failed on a G5, and the advice to an owner with a dead machine was to pull the drive and move on rather than repair it. One opinion, but it matches the visual-inspection caveat above. Source

Before you start

  • Boards changed during production. Check your board number against the guides before you order parts.
  • A mains power supply can hold a dangerous charge. If you are not trained to work on one, have a professional do it.
