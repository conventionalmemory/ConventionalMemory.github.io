The Sierra shelf

Two people at a kitchen table in 1980 start a company that makes adventure games. Four decades later, you can read how it went.

Connie: “Zack Zip is the neighbor kid, and he is in every adventure party. He has read the Sierra books and says he was in them. He was not.”

Not All Fairy Tales Have Happy Endings

Ken Williams · 2020 · Lulu.com · 408 pages

The co-founder tells the story of Sierra from his side of the table: the hits, the growth, the sale and what went wrong after. Roberta is in the story everywhere, and so are the lessons.

Once Upon a Point and Click

David L. Craddock · 2017 · Self-published · 156 pages

Sierra's adventure games from the designers' side, with Roberta Williams and Jane Jensen at the center, from King's Quest to Gabriel Knight.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

Hackers: Heroes of the Computer Revolution

Steven Levy · 1984 · Anchor Press/Doubleday

From MIT labs to home computers and the game makers of the early 80s. Still the best book about hacker culture.

Doom, id and the shooters

Two guys, one 386 and a lot of pizza. These books cover the people, the code and the cultural earthquake.

Connie: “Emma 386 says Doom ran on a 386, so these books are basically about her. Nobody has been brave enough to disagree.”

Masters of Doom

David Kushner · 2003 · Random House · 352 pages

How John Carmack and John Romero built id Software, made Doom and changed games, and how the friendship came apart. The best-loved book on the subject.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

Doom Guy: Life in First Person

John Romero · 2023 · Abrams Press · 370 pages

The memoir from the co-creator of Doom himself, from a kid learning to program on Apple IIs to one of the most influential games of the 90s.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

Game Engine Black Book: DOOM

Fabien Sanglard · 2018

For the programmers: how Doom's engine actually works, with diagrams. Pair it with the Wolfenstein 3D black book by the same author.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

Rocket Jump: Quake and the Golden Age of First-Person Shooters

David L. Craddock · 2019 · Self-published · 395 pages

The making of Quake, and how a team that had just made Doom took on 3D. Honest, detailed and a very good read.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

Stairway to Badass: The Making and Remaking of Doom

David L. Craddock · 2018 · Self-published · 202 pages

The story of the original Doom and the 2016 return, with the people who made both.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

Blizzard and Diablo

A dungeon, a town of people who really need your help and, somewhere, a secret cow level.

Connie: “Nibble has read the cow level chapter nine times. He says it is nothing like his wheel. He says he has not been back since.”

Stay Awhile and Listen, Book I

David L. Craddock · 2013 · DM Press (Digital Monument Press) · 274 pages

The story of Blizzard and Blizzard North, and how Diablo came together, from the people who built it.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

Stay Awhile and Listen, Book II

David L. Craddock · 2014

The sequel: Diablo II, the Battle.net years and everything that made the series a legend. Yes, the cow level is in here.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

Consoles, Nintendo and Atari

Before the 90s PC took over the living room, there was a box under the TV. These are the best books about it.

Connie: “Sandy Blaster takes sides in every console war, loudly, on IRQ 5. She says Sega did it better. Nobody has asked her.”

Console Wars

Blake J. Harris · 2014 · HarperCollins · 558 pages

Sega against Nintendo in the 90s, told like a thriller. It reads fast, and it is a lot of fun.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

Game Over: How Nintendo Zapped an American Industry

David Sheff · 1993 · Random House · 445 pages

The classic account of how Nintendo took over the American market, written while it was still happening.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

The Ultimate History of Video Games

Steven L. Kent · 2001 · Three Rivers Press · 624 pages

Pong to PlayStation, with first-hand interviews. The standard history of the industry, and a great reference.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

Replay: The History of Video Games

Tristan Donovan · 2010 · Yellow Ant · 513 pages

A worldwide history that does not forget the computers, the arcades or the people who made them.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

Racing the Beam

Nick Montfort and Ian Bogost · 2009 · MIT Press · 184 pages

How programmers made the Atari 2600 do things it was never meant to do, game by game. For anyone who loves clever hardware tricks.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

Atari Inc.: Business Is Fun

Marty Goldberg and Curt Vendel · 2012 · Syzygy Press · 800 pages

A big, thorough history of Atari from the inside, from Pong through the crash.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

Super Mario: How Nintendo Conquered America

Jeff Ryan · 2011 · Portfolio · 304 pages

Nintendo's history, told through Mario and the people around him. Light, funny and quick.

How games actually get made

Behind every great game there is a crunch, a miracle, and a build that would not compile. Here is the real story.

Connie: “Hiram says he has read all of these from the loft, where no one can see him. He says he has also written a game. We have not seen that either.”

Blood, Sweat, and Pixels

Jason Schreier · 2017 · Harper

Ten stories of how games get made, in all the chaos. Readers keep recommending it, and it is easy to see why.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

Press Reset

Jason Schreier · 2021 · Grand Central Publishing · 320 pages

The other side: studios that closed, and the people who started again. Honest and hard to put down.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

The Making of Prince of Persia: Journals 1985-1993

Jordan Mechner · 2011 · Self-published

The designer's own diary while he was making Karateka and Prince of Persia. It is as close as you can get to being in the room.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

ZZT

A Boss Fight Books look at the little DOS game editor that taught a generation to make games. A must for anyone who loved MegaZeux.

Game design and virtual worlds

What makes a game fun, what makes a world feel alive, and where multiplayer came from.

A Theory of Fun for Game Design

Raph Koster · 2004 · Paraglyph Press

Raph Koster, a lead designer of Ultima Online, explains why games are fun and why they stop being fun. Illustrated with cartoons and full of ideas. Everyone who makes games should read it.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

The rise of computer game culture, from Dungeons and Dragons and Richard Garriott's Ultima to LAN parties and online worlds.

Check Amazon · Check eBay

A huge history of how Dungeons and Dragons and role-playing games came to be. The roots of every dungeon you have crawled.
