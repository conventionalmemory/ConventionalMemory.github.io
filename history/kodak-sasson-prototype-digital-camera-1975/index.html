Kodak Sasson Prototype Digital Camera (1975)

Steven Sasson's self-contained electronic camera built at Eastman Kodak, the first handheld digital camera.

Built by Kodak engineer Steven Sasson around a monochrome Fairchild CCD, it recorded to a cassette tape and was viewed on a separate playback unit attached to a TV. It was a lab prototype that never went on sale, but it showed that a self-contained filmless camera was possible. Kodak's later DCS and DC lines grew from the same research lineage, which is why collectors treat it as the starting point of the timeline.
