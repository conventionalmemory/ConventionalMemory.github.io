Clean and lubricate a floppy drive that will not read

Dried grease and dust are a common reason an old floppy drive struggles. A careful clean of the heads and rails, with a tiny amount of fresh lubricant in the right places, often brings a drive back before any parts are replaced.

Drive · Medium · about 75 minutes. Worth doing. Reports say this fix helps on many machines.

You are in the right place if: The drive clicks, refuses to read disks, throws read errors, or the head carriage moves stiffly.

Before you start

  • Do not over-oil. Excess lubricant can reach the heads and the media.
  • Do not bend the head arm or push on the heads.
  • Unplug the machine before taking the drive out. Keep fingers away from any mains parts in external drive cases.

Board revisions and models

Drives differ: 5.25 inch, 3.5 inch and the Commodore and Apple designs each have their own lubrication points. Follow a model-specific guide.

Step by step

  1. Check the disk and cable first Try a known-good disk and check the data and power connectors, because a bad disk or cable is the cheapest fault to rule out.
  2. Power off and remove the drive Disconnect everything and take the drive out of the machine, noting how the cables go on.
  3. Photograph the drive Take photos of the head carriage, the spindle and any springs or belts before touching anything.
  4. Clean the heads Wipe the heads gently with a swab lightly dampened with isopropyl alcohol, using a light touch and no pressure that could bend the head arm.
  5. Clean the rails and carriage Remove old, gummy grease from the guide rails with a swab and alcohol, and check the carriage slides freely by hand.
  6. Clean the spindle and clamp Wipe the disk clamping area and spindle hub so disks seat flat.
  7. Add a tiny amount of lubricant Apply a very small drop of suitable light lubricant only to the rails or points a drive guide names. Keep it away from the heads and any belt.
  8. Reassemble and refit Put the drive back together, reconnect the cables and power, and check it spins and seeks.
  9. Test with a disk Read and write a scratch disk and run a copy or directory several times.
  10. Look at belts and capacitors if it still fails A stretched belt or failing capacitor is the next suspect; see the belt and recap guides.
