Fix a Sega CD tray that will not move: belt, limiter switch and helix

The Model 1 Sega CD is known for bad tray belts, dirty limiter switches and worn helix screws. A belt swap and some cleaning fixes most tray problems.

Belt · Careful · about 90 minutes. Worth doing. Reports say this fix helps on many machines.

You are in the right place if: The tray will not open or close, the disc does not spin, or games never load.

Before you start

  • A blown fuse (F301 on a Mega-CD 2) can mimic a dead drive. Check it before diving into the tray.
  • Model 1 drives can be fragile. Do not force the tray.
  • A Sega CD attached to a Genesis draws a lot from the Genesis supply.

Board revisions and models

Model 1 uses Sony or JVC drives. Model 2 uses a Funai Sony KSS-210A or Samsung SOH-O4T, or a Sega board with a JVC Optima-6 or Sony KSS-240A. Model 2 is far more robust, and its common fault is a blown fuse.

Step by step

  1. Identify your model Model 1 is front-loading and Model 2 is top-loading. Model 1 is the fussier one.
  2. Disconnect and unplug Remove the Sega CD from the Genesis and unplug power.
  3. Open the unit Remove the case screws and photograph the drive and tray mechanism.
  4. Inspect the belt Check for stretching, cracks or slipping. A warped or stiff belt means it is time to replace it.
  5. Replace the belt Fit the correct size belt for your drive without twisting it.
  6. Clean the limiter switches Wipe the tray limiter switches with isopropyl alcohol or contact cleaner and test them with your multimeter.
  7. Check the helix screw On Sony drives, inspect the Archimedes (helix) screw and its follower for wear.
  8. Clean and lubricate Clean the lens and lightly lubricate moving parts.
  9. Reassemble and cycle the tray Run the tray in and out repeatedly before putting a disc in.
  10. Test with an official disc Use a genuine disc to check reading, not a burned one.

Tips, tales and maybes

Found in write-ups and forum threads. These are other people's reports, not tested by us.

  • Tip (Medium confidence). ConsoleMods describes the Model 1 as plagued by bad tray belts, dirty limiter switches, faulty helix screws and leaking capacitors. Source
  • Story (Medium confidence). One Model 2 with no signs of life only needed a new F301 fuse. Source
  • Story (Low confidence). A Model 1 owner replaced belt, battery, laser and helix gear, then got reliable reading after a multimeter laser adjustment. Source
  • Maybe (Low confidence). Graphics glitches only in FMV on a Model 1 may be Genesis voltage regulators rather than the Sega CD. This was one video creator's experience. Source
