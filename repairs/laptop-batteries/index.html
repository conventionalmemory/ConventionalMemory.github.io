Repair guide

Revive or replace the battery in a vintage laptop

Many early laptops depend on a sealed rechargeable pack plus a smaller backup battery, and both age badly. This guide covers testing, replacing the backup cell and deciding whether to recell or replace the main pack.

You are in the right place if: The laptop dies the moment mains is removed, or the clock and setup reset, or the pack is swollen or crusty.

Before you start

  • Do not charge a swollen, leaking or damaged pack. Lithium cells can catch fire.
  • Never short a battery pack, and never charge cells of the wrong chemistry.
  • Recelling a pack involves spot welding and charge-control circuits. If you are not experienced, buy a rebuilt pack instead.
  • If the laptop has a plasma or CRT display, do not open the display section.

Board revisions and models

Pack construction varies widely: Macintosh Portable and PowerBook 100 use different packs and backup cells. Follow a model-specific guide.

Step by step

  1. Remove the main pack first Power the machine off, disconnect mains and take the pack out. Look at it: any swelling, leaking or crusty terminals means it should not be used.
  2. Identify the cells Note the chemistry and voltage on the label of the pack (NiCd, NiMH, lead-acid or lithium) before buying anything.
  3. Test on mains only With the pack removed, see whether the laptop runs from its adapter. If it does not, the battery is not the only problem.
  4. Find the backup or clock battery Locate the small backup cell on the board. Open the case following your model's disassembly guide.
  5. Check for leak damage Look for crust or corrosion near backup cells and clean any residue with gloves on, using a mild acid for alkaline leaks, then isopropyl alcohol.
  6. Replace the backup cell Fit a matching cell or socket it, with correct polarity.
  7. Decide recell or replace Compare cost: a ready-made replacement pack is simplest, while recelling means safely rebuilding the pack with new cells of the right chemistry.
  8. Charge under supervision Charge a new pack only on the original charger, on a hard surface, and check it does not get hot.
  9. Test the system Check that clock and setup survive a power cycle and that the machine runs on battery for a short test.
  10. Dispose of old cells properly Take old packs to battery recycling.
