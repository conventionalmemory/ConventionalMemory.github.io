Reseat socketed chips and clean oxidised legs

Older machines use socketed chips whose legs oxidise and creep out. Reseating chips and cleaning the legs can bring a computer back with no soldering.

Open the interactive checklist Tick off each step as you go. It saves in your browser.

Memory · Medium · about 60 minutes. Worth doing. Reports say this fix helps on many machines.

You are in the right place if: The machine is dead, shows garbage or has random crashes that move around.

Before you start

  • Chips can be static sensitive. Ground yourself.
  • Do not pry chips with screwdrivers; use a chip puller.
  • Check notch orientation before power on; a reversed chip can be destroyed.

Board revisions and models

Some chips are soldered. Only reseat chips that are in sockets.

Step by step

  1. Power off and unplug Disconnect power and any peripherals.
  2. Photograph the board Take photos of all socketed chips and their notch directions.
  3. Mark orientation Note which end each chip's notch points.
  4. Lift chips gently Use a chip puller to lift each chip evenly, working from alternate ends.
  5. Inspect the legs Look for oxidised or bent legs.
  6. Clean the legs Wipe legs with isopropyl alcohol and a soft brush.
  7. Clean the sockets Blow out the sockets and clean with a swab.
  8. Straighten bent legs Straighten gently with a flat tool and fit one row at a time.
  9. Refit chips Check orientation and press the chip fully into the socket.
  10. Test Power up and test the machine.
