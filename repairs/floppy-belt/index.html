Replace a stretched or broken drive belt

Some old drives turn the spindle through a rubber belt that stretches or perishes with age. Replacing it is a mechanical job with a cheap part, once you have checked that your drive is a belt-driven model.

Drive · Medium · about 60 minutes. Try it, but check first. This will not fix every machine, so read the sources first.

You are in the right place if: The disk spins slowly, wobbles, stops, or the drive makes a whirring sound but never reads.

Before you start

  • Do not stretch the belt on to the pulley with pliers; slip it on gently.
  • Keep lubricant away from belts and pulleys.
  • If your drive has a mains-powered internal supply, unplug it and do not open that section.

Board revisions and models

Belt dimensions, routing and whether a belt exists at all vary by model and revision.

Step by step

  1. Confirm the drive uses a belt Check a disassembly guide or service manual for your drive: not every model turns the spindle by belt.
  2. Power off and remove the drive Unplug everything and take the drive out, noting cables and screws.
  3. Open the mechanism Remove the cover and any shield, and take photos of the belt path before moving anything.
  4. Inspect the old belt Look for cracks, stretch or a sticky, melted belt. A belt that has turned to goo needs thorough cleaning of the pulleys.
  5. Clean pulleys and spindle Clean both pulleys with isopropyl alcohol so the new belt can grip.
  6. Measure and choose the new belt Match the belt's width and circumference (measure the old belt or use the listed size) and buy a belt made for your drive.
  7. Fit the new belt Slip the belt over the spindle and the motor pulley, making sure it sits in the groove without twisting.
  8. Reassemble and spin test Put the drive back together and check that the disk spins smoothly at speed.
  9. Check alignment and speed If the drive has a speed adjustment, follow the maker's procedure; otherwise test with disks.
