Replace a dead SCSI hard drive with a SCSI2SD or ZuluSCSI

A SCSI2SD or ZuluSCSI board presents an SD card to the Mac as a SCSI drive. It is a practical way to bring a vintage SCSI Mac back to life without hunting for scarce old drives.

Open the interactive checklist Tick off each step as you go. It saves in your browser.

Drive · Medium · about 90 minutes. Worth doing. Reports say this fix helps on many machines.

You are in the right place if: The Mac clicks, will not spin up, or never mounts its hard drive, and you want a silent replacement.

Before you start

  • Compact Macs contain a CRT with dangerous stored voltage. Do not open past the logic board area.
  • Unplug before opening and handle power connectors with care.

Board revisions and models

Connector type and SCSI ID differ between models. Check your Mac's SCSI notes.

Step by step

  1. Back up what you can If the old drive still mounts, copy data off first.
  2. Choose a SCSI emulator Buy a SCSI2SD or ZuluSCSI that matches your machine's connector.
  3. Prepare the SD card Format the SD card and set up the device ID and size using the board's utility.
  4. Install a system image Prepare a bootable system image for your Mac model and copy it to the card.
  5. Power off and open the Mac Unplug and open the case up to the drive bay only.
  6. Remove the old drive Disconnect SCSI and power cables and unscrew the drive.
  7. Fit the emulator Mount the board with brackets, connect SCSI and power, and set the ID.
  8. Check termination Make sure the SCSI chain has termination where required.
  9. Boot and test Power on, boot the Mac and check the emulated volume mounts.
