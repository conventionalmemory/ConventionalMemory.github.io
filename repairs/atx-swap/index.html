Repair guide

Replace a dead vintage PC power supply with an ATX unit

Original AT and XT supplies are hard to find and often need serious work. Many owners swap the whole unit for a modern supply, or a compact unit like a Meanwell, and keep the original for display.

Power · Careful · about 90 minutes. Worth doing. Reports say this fix helps on many machines.

You are in the right place if: The PC is dead or unstable, or the original supply smells, hums or has failed.

Before you start

  • Never open the old supply. Mains-side capacitors hold dangerous charge.
  • Check polarity on every connector before you power on.
  • If you are unsure, hire a repair person for the supply swap.

Board revisions and models

AT, XT, PS/2 and portable supplies differ in connectors and rails.

Step by step

  1. Unplug and open the PC Disconnect from mains and open the case.
  2. Label the cables Photograph the cabling and label each connector.
  3. Remove the old supply Unscrew and lift it out without opening it.
  4. Plan the replacement Choose a modern unit or an adapter harness suited to your machine.
  5. Check the voltages needed Check which rails your machine uses (including -5V and -12V) and whether your chosen supply provides them.
  6. Fit the new supply Mount it and connect the cables with the correct polarity.
  7. Add a load if needed Some supplies need a minimum load to start.
  8. Test off the machine first Test the supply on the bench with a load, checking voltages.
  9. Power up and monitor Power on the PC and watch for smoke, smells or noise.
  10. Keep the original Store the old supply safely.
