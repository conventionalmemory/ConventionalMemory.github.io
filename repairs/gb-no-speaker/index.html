Repair guide

Game Boy has sound in headphones but not the speaker

The headphone jack contains a switch that cuts the speaker when you plug in. If it sticks, the console thinks headphones are always connected.

Open the interactive checklist Tick off each step as you go. It saves in your browser.

Diagnosis · Medium · about 45 minutes. Worth doing. Reports say this fix helps on many machines.

You are in the right place if: Audio works through headphones but not through the internal speaker.

Before you start

  • Do not bend contacts more than needed.
  • Check for corroded traces and cold joints while inside.

Board revisions and models

The jack-switch write-up covers a DMG-001. Check other models separately before assuming the same fault.

Step by step

  1. Confirm the symptom Plug in headphones. If sound works there but not on the speaker, continue.
  2. Check the volume wheel Rotate it fully to rule out a dirty wheel.
  3. Open the console Remove the case screws and separate the halves.
  4. Test the speaker Measure its coil with a multimeter. Open means it is dead.
  5. Inspect the jack Check the jack's contact switch.
  6. Clean the contacts Clean them thoroughly and scrape gently to improve conductivity.
  7. Straighten the contact Gently bend it back into alignment.
  8. Test continuity Confirm the switch reads closed with no plug inserted.
  9. Replace the speaker only if dead Fit a new speaker if the coil is open.
  10. Reassemble and test Check the speaker volume.

Tips, tales and maybes

Found in write-ups and forum threads. These are other people's reports, not tested by us.

  • Story (Medium confidence). On a DMG-001 the speaker was fine but the jack's contact switch was faulty, so the machine thought headphones were always plugged in. Source
  • Story (Medium confidence). A Game Boy Pocket had a dead speaker that was replaced with a larger spare. Source
