Coax a stuck vintage hard drive into spinning up

Old hard drives can seize when the heads stick to the platters or the spindle grease hardens. Before trying anything, work out whether the drive is worth saving, copy what you can off, and accept that the data may not survive.

You are in the right place if: The drive powers up but the platters do not spin, or it hums, buzzes or clicks without ever reaching speed.

Before you start

  • Do not open the sealed drive housing. Dust ruins platters and will not be recoverable.
  • Drives that spin up may fail again within minutes, so copy first.
  • If the data is valuable, stop and use a professional recovery service.

Board revisions and models

Stiction affects very old half-height drives and some late-1990s models differently. Methods vary and no single trick works for every drive.

Step by step

  1. Decide if the data matters If the drive holds irreplaceable data, stop here and consider a professional data recovery service.
  2. Check power and cables first Test the supply rails and try a known-good cable and controller. A weak PSU can look like a stuck drive.
  3. Listen and feel Power the drive briefly in a safe position and listen for spin-up noises. Do not leave it powered if it buzzes.
  4. Check that the spindle can turn With power off, try to feel whether a stuck spindle moves by hand where the drive design allows it.
  5. Try a gentle spin-up assist Hold the drive level and give a gentle twist along the platter axis as you power it, a technique often reported by hobbyists; stop if nothing happens.
  6. Copy data immediately If it spins, copy everything straight away before shutting down or moving it.
  7. Check the filesystem Run a read-only check or image the drive instead of writing to it.
  8. Retire the drive Once data is copied, retire the drive or use it as a display piece.
  9. Record what happened Note model and symptoms for others.

Tips, tales and maybes

Found in write-ups and forum threads. These are other people's reports, not tested by us.

  • Maybe (Low confidence). Some hobbyists report that a gentle rotational twist at power-up frees a seized spindle; results are inconsistent and the risk of damage is real. General technique, no source.
  • Watch out (Low confidence). Never tap or knock a stuck drive hard to free it. You can score the platters. General technique, no source.
