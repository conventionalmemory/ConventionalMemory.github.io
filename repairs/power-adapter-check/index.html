Repair guide

Match, test and swap a console power adapter safely

Most no-power problems start at the wall adapter and its plug, not the console. This flow rules the adapter in or out without opening anything that carries mains voltage.

Power · Easy · about 15 minutes. Worth doing. Reports say this fix helps on many machines.

You are in the right place if: The console is dead or reboots, and you suspect the power brick, cord or plug.

Before you start

  • Mains power supplies hold lethal voltage. ConsoleMods says never open a recently powered supply and never debug one while it is plugged in.
  • Do not mix supplies between regions with different mains voltages.
  • If the console still fails with a known-good adapter, stop here and move on to the dead console flow.

Board revisions and models

The Dreamcast has eight revisions of internal PSU across VA0 to VA2 boards. The Famicom Disk System takes a DC supply, and one owner reported center-negative polarity, so check the label before connecting anything.

Step by step

  1. Read the labels Note the voltage, current, AC or DC, and the polarity symbol on both the console input and the adapter. They must agree.
  2. Check the region Never run a console on an adapter or internal supply built for a different mains voltage. A mismatch can destroy the main filter capacitor.
  3. Inspect cord and plug Look for cuts, kinks, heat marks, bent pins and a loose barrel. Replace any adapter with damaged insulation.
  4. Clean the connector With everything unplugged, wipe the plug and the console jack with isopropyl alcohol on a swab, then let them dry fully.
  5. Swap in a known-good adapter Use one with exactly the same voltage, polarity and at least the same current. If the console wakes up, the old adapter is the culprit.
  6. Know your model's quirks The North American SNES uses a unique plug so NES adapters will not fit. N64 uses an external brick that plugs into the console. Dreamcast and Saturn have internal supplies.
  7. Consider a modern replacement For internal-supply consoles, drop-in third-party supplies exist for the Dreamcast and Saturn. Choose a known maker and match the listed model.
  8. Retire the bad adapter Do not open a mains adapter to repair it. Replace it, and keep the old one out of circulation.

Tips, tales and maybes

Found in write-ups and forum threads. These are other people's reports, not tested by us.

  • Watch out (Medium confidence). A PS1 owner killed the main filter capacitor by running an NTSC-J console on a PAL power supply. Check the mains rating first. Source
  • Tip (Medium confidence). ConsoleMods lists repairing the stock Dreamcast supply as the preferred route, with modern external or internal replacements as the fallback. Source
  • Tip (Medium confidence). N64 bricks fail in several ways, including open fuses, broken joints and failed rectifiers. These are internal, mains-side repairs. Source
