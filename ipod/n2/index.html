iPod Bench

iPod nano (2nd generation)

An anodized aluminum bar with its flash memory soldered to the logic board. There is no storage to swap, but the battery can be.

Open the interactive page All iPods

What is inside

  1. Top bezel (with hold switch). Pry it off with an opening tool in the seam against the metal body. Lock the hold switch first.
  2. Bottom bezel. Same method. It surrounds the dock connector and headphone jack.
  3. Aluminum body. Screws come out, then the inside slides free.
  4. Click wheel and headphone jack. One assembly joined by a fragile ribbon. Do not detach that ribbon all the way.
  5. Logic board with flash memory. The LCD ribbon needs a metal bracket removed and a black retaining bar lifted. The dock connector has two Phillips #00 screws, one each side.
  6. Battery. Soldered. iFixit's guide has you desolder three connections.

Numbers follow the order a repairer meets the parts in a published teardown. A square means we have not confirmed the order. Drawn by us, not to scale.

Quick facts

  • Model: A1199. 2GB, 4GB and 8GB. Launched September 2006
  • Chip: Samsung S5L8701, the first Samsung chip in an iPod
  • Screen: 176 x 132 color LCD, about 1.5 inches
  • Storage: Samsung NAND flash on the logic board, not a module you can unplug
  • Battery: 3.7 V lithium polymer, about 370 mAh by one teardown's estimate (another source says 400). It is soldered at three points.
  • Codec: Wolfson WM8975 per The Apple Wiki and Rockbox. One teardown reads WM8750S, so we call it unsettled.

How it opens

  1. Lock the hold switch.
  2. Pry the top bezel off with a plastic opening tool, then the bottom bezel the same way.
  3. Remove the screws, then unplug the cable between the logic board and the click wheel and headphone-jack assembly.
  4. Push the logic board assembly out. The click wheel and jack stay in the case.

Tools named in the guides: Plastic opening tool, spudger, Phillips #00 and #000, tweezers, soldering iron for the battery.

Change the storage

  • There is no flash swap. The memory is soldered on, and the only chip-swap project we found is for the 3rd generation nano.

Rockbox

Supported. Rockbox lists the nano 1st and 2nd generations with no major issues. We could not read the install page, so check the live Rockbox wiki for the steps.

What goes wrong

  • Blank white screen: A recurring theme in iFixit's answers. A new display or logic board is the listed fix.
  • Won't power on or charge: Battery.
  • Click wheel or center button unresponsive: The click wheel is part of the case assembly on this model.
  • Hold switch will not lock the buttons: iFixit has a hold-switch cable guide.

Mods that are documented for it

  • New battery. The cheapest fix for a short day. Match the capacity to your exact model and size, because the 60GB and 80GB video and the thick classic take a thicker battery. (iFixit parts: iPod batteries)
  • Rockbox firmware, dual boot. Open-source firmware that plays FLAC, Opus and more. The original software stays one boot away. Non-classic models need the iPod formatted as FAT32. (Rockbox, Rockbox install manual (mirror))
