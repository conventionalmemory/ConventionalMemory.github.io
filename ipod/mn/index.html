iPod Bench

iPod mini

An aluminum tube with a Microdrive inside. It has no snap-fit halves: the plastic ends come off and the whole inside slides out.

Open the interactive page All iPods

What is inside

  1. Aluminum body. The outer tube. Everything slides out of it.
  2. Top and bottom plastic end bezels. Pried away with a small flat tool. A hair dryer on the adhesive helps.
  3. Metal retaining bracket. Pushed out once the bezels are off.
  4. Click wheel and its orange ribbon. Unplug the ribbon from the logic board.
  5. Headphone jack board. Two Phillips screws. Then push at the bottom of the click wheel to slide the whole assembly out.
  6. Logic board. The battery sits on it and plugs in at a plain connector. Pull the connector, never the wires.
  7. Battery. Plugs into the logic board.
  8. Microdrive on its orange ribbon. Do not unplug the drive cable at the logic-board end. One modder caused a Sad iPod that way.

Numbers follow the order a repairer meets the parts in a published teardown. A square means we have not confirmed the order. Drawn by us, not to scale.

Quick facts

  • Model: A1051 for both generations. EMC 1984 (1st) and 2044 (2nd). Launched January 2004 and February 2005
  • Screen: 1.67 inch monochrome, blue backlight
  • Storage: A 1 inch Microdrive, 4,200 rpm, ATA-66. 4GB in the 1st generation, 4GB or 6GB in the 2nd.
  • Battery: 450 mAh in the 1st generation and 650 mAh in the 2nd, both lithium polymer, per The Apple Wiki. iFixit sells one for each (EC007-3 and EC007-5).
  • Rating: Apple rated 8 hours (1st) and up to 18 hours (2nd)

How it opens

  1. Pry the top and bottom plastic end bezels away from the aluminum body with a small flat tool, warming the adhesive with a hair dryer.
  2. Push out the metal retaining bracket.
  3. Unplug the orange click-wheel ribbon from the logic board.
  4. Remove the two Phillips screws holding the headphone jack.
  5. Push at the bottom of the click wheel to slide the internal assembly out.

Tools named in the guides: Small flat driver, plastic picks, hair dryer, Phillips screwdriver.

Change the storage

  • Of the iFlash family, only the iFlash SD-CF adapter fits the mini. The Solo, Dual, Quad and CF boards list the mini as not compatible.
  • A CompactFlash card in place of the Microdrive also works. One tester reported cards from 512MB to 32GB working, suggests iPod firmware 1.4.1 first, and warns against unplugging the drive cable at the logic-board end.

Rockbox

Stable. Rockbox lists the iPod mini (1st and 2nd generation) with no major issues, and the install manual lists it as supported.

What goes wrong

  • Sad iPod icon: A dead Microdrive, or cracked solder joints at the drive connector. A CompactFlash swap is the usual cure.
  • Short battery life: Replace the battery. EveryMac calls the job difficult but says no soldering is needed.
  • Crackly jack: The headphone jack board has its own iFixit guide.

Mods that are documented for it

  • Flash storage swap. Replace the spinning drive with a memory card or an SSD. It is silent, runs cooler and wakes faster. Which board you need depends on the model, so check the model page. (iFlash)
  • New battery. The cheapest fix for a short day. Match the capacity to your exact model and size, because the 60GB and 80GB video and the thick classic take a thicker battery. (iFixit parts: iPod batteries)
  • New headphone jack and hold switch. For a crackly jack or a stuck hold slider. On the video the hold switch's plastic part may need moving to the new jack. (iFixit guides)
  • Click wheel replacement. For a wheel that skips or goes dead. On the classic it is the last layer of the stack and a long job. (iFixit: iPod Classic Click Wheel Replacement)
  • Rockbox firmware, dual boot. Open-source firmware that plays FLAC, Opus and more. The original software stays one boot away. Non-classic models need the iPod formatted as FAT32. (Rockbox, Rockbox install manual (mirror))
