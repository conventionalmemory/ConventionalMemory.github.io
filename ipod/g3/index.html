iPod Bench

iPod 3rd generation

Four touch buttons in a row above a touch wheel, and the first dock connector. It opens like a book, from the right edge.

Open the interactive page All iPods

What is inside

  1. Metal rear case. Opens last, like the back cover of a book. Lock the hold switch first.
  2. Headphone-jack ribbon. The first thing to unplug. Its connector is unusually tall, so do not pull the plastic base off the ribbon.
  3. Hard drive and its ribbon. 1.8 inch drive on a flat ribbon.
  4. Battery. Unplugs at a white connector. It lifts out of the front section late in the job.
  5. Logic board. Six T6 Torx screws hold it. The orange touch-wheel cable comes off first.
  6. Display. Comes away on its own orange ribbon once the logic board is out.
  7. Front panel. Carries the touch wheel and button row. Last to come apart.

Numbers follow the order a repairer meets the parts in a published teardown. A square means we have not confirmed the order. Drawn by us, not to scale.

Quick facts

  • Model: A1040, EMC 1961. Launched April 2003
  • Storage: 1.8 inch Toshiba hard drive, 4,200 rpm, 10 to 40GB over the model run
  • Battery: Sources disagree on the original (630 and 850 mAh are both quoted). iFixit sells an 850 mAh replacement. It plugs in with a white connector.
  • Case: Plastic front, metal back

How it opens

  1. Lock the hold switch.
  2. Slide an opening pick into the seam between the plastic front and the metal back at the right edge.
  3. Rock the pick along the right edge to release the five retaining tabs.
  4. Open the two halves like a book and unplug the headphone-jack ribbon first.

Tools named in the guides: Plastic opening pick, spudger, T6 Torx driver.

Change the storage

  • The 4th Gen iPod to iFlash converter fits the 3rd generation, so iFlash Solo, Dual, Quad and CF can go in through it. iPodWiki says some options can stress the drive ribbon, and that the iFlash-ATA1 board supports the 3rd and 4th generations directly.

Rockbox

Stable. Rockbox lists it with no major issues.

What goes wrong

  • Touch wheel not responding: The touch-wheel cable has its own iFixit guide, so reseat or replace that first.
  • Sad iPod icon, or a folder with an exclamation mark: Usually the hard drive. Replace the drive or fit a flash adapter.
  • Won't hold a charge: Replace the battery.

Mods that are documented for it

  • Flash storage swap. Replace the spinning drive with a memory card or an SSD. It is silent, runs cooler and wakes faster. Which board you need depends on the model, so check the model page. (iFlash)
  • New battery. The cheapest fix for a short day. Match the capacity to your exact model and size, because the 60GB and 80GB video and the thick classic take a thicker battery. (iFixit parts: iPod batteries)
  • Rockbox firmware, dual boot. Open-source firmware that plays FLAC, Opus and more. The original software stays one boot away. Non-classic models need the iPod formatted as FAT32. (Rockbox, Rockbox install manual (mirror))
